H3 dialogue continuity and speaker structure rules (v5)
- Descriptive prose is English; protected speech keeps its exact original language, punctuation and words.
- A speech event has ONE introduction: its speaker identity, delivery, any assigned voice reference, then its protected utterance token. Do not nest a second says/speaks introduction inside begins to speak, delivers the line, finishes the sentence or responds. Remove repeated voice explanations around the same utterance without changing the voice assignment.
- Keep source voice references attached to the same speaker. Scene characters, source labels and reference roles cannot be reassigned during review.
- Each supplied line is spoken once unless the caller explicitly requests repetition. A line crossing a cut is one continuous utterance; use protected ordered parts if provided, with the same speaker and paired <scenetrans> continuity markers. Do not restart the whole line on the listening shot.
- A short line does not license invented words to fill a shot. Once a supplied line finishes, describe the listener or a nonverbal reaction; do not leave a direction to keep talking beyond the supplied words. Honor requested off-screen continuation without adding speech.
- Preserve caller-specified cut times and action constraints. Do not force each short line to fill an entire long shot. Timing not fixed by the caller may be adjusted to support the supplied speech, without changing total duration. Keep <d> content distinct from directions and visible lettering.
- All protected tokens are opaque. Never put delivery directions, speaker identities or another <d> wrapper inside a token. Return only the complete corrected H3 document, with no explanation.

- Before writing, mentally map each supplied utterance to its speaker, its shot or consecutive shot parts, and the point where it ENDS. A whole utterance token consumes the entire line. A later shot must not say that speaker keeps speaking or finishes that same line unless that later shot contains its remaining protected part. Never spend the whole line before a cut and invent a continuation after it.
- A requested mid-sentence cut needs an explicit protected partial utterance on EACH side of that cut, not a full line followed by an empty "continues speaking" direction. Preserve the original ordering and concatenate to the original line exactly. After the final part, close that speech event and use silent actions until another supplied utterance starts.
- The caller's constraints outrank the draft. Shot count, cut timings and performance invented by the writer may be changed to repair a contradictory speech schedule. Do not preserve a mistaken draft merely because mechanical findings are empty. Never change caller-pinned times, shot counts or actions.

- Cross-cut speech boundary placement: the first shot ends with its protected speech part followed by <scenetrans>. Do not append an English continuity explanation after that closing marker. In the following shot, put the continuity explanation BEFORE its connecting <scenetrans> and speaker/utterance introduction. Preserve the requested audio continuity, speaker identity and all protected parts; relocate the explanation, never replace it with invented speech.

- Audit speech time separately from total video time using the supplied scheduling evidence. Never treat a long video as a requirement for continuous conversation. Make the end of each speech event and the subsequent nonverbal action explicit. Ordinary speech-duration estimates are advisory and never override caller-pinned timing or expressive delivery.

- Articulation descriptions do not license a second introduction of the same utterance. For example, "S2 responds, his lips forming the words clearly, S2 says: TOKEN" must become ONE introduction, retaining delivery and the same voice reference. Do not delete a different speaker or a separate utterance, and do not put staging inside a protected token.
- Source voice-timbre reference duration is metadata about the source recording, not a timing instruction for target dialogue. Do not extend target speech to that duration. Preserve explicit audio reuse and synchronization requests.
